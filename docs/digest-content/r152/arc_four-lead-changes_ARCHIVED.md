## The Season Arc: Four Lead Changes, One Clean Champion

Ten rounds, 99 songs, 766 votes, four lead changes. It began in chaos and ended in arithmetic. Darren Paletz opened Round 1 by accidentally group-calling the entire league, going dark, submitting Rise's 'Do You Know Him?' at 5am under a proof-of-life demand, and winning anyway at 11 points. Preparation, established immediately, was for cowards.

Mashew struck first in Round 2, seizing the lead at 23 with Polo & Pan's 'Pays imaginaire' — 14 points and an early thesis statement that this league would reward curation and annihilate the obvious (see: Bocelli, −2, same round). But DP wasn't done. He reclaimed the top through Boner Jamz and the Target round, winning the former outright with Devin The Dude and reaching 40 cumulative points by Round 4 on pure quiet accumulation. Arrive late, deposit, leave.

Round 5 was the season's true hinge. Jonathan Black submitted The Be Good Tanyas' 'Waiting Around To Die' for 15 points — the year's second-highest single-song score — and took the lead at 47. In the same round, DP filed no ballot, absorbed the −5 no-vote penalty on Sir Mix-A-Lot's 'Posse On Broadway,' and dropped off the pace for good. JB held through Surrender Monkeys and Stranger Danger, but the room's most-loved song of the year was already sitting in Mashew's column: 'Watermelon' at 17 unanimous points in Round 7.

Round 8, Couple of Lines, was the takeover. Mashew placed THE BOBBY LEES' 'Bellevue' at 13 while JB's entry stalled, and the lead flipped to Mashew at 71. He never gave it back. Round 9 was the quietest of the season — Steiny won 'Plot So Thicc' with Al Wilson's 'The Snake' at 7 points, said nothing all week, was misattributed by JB, and got congratulated by no one — and Mashew simply held. Round 10 closed the ledger: Jimmy Troy won the finale with Refused's 'New Noise' at 14, but the cumulative math was settled. Mashew finished at 80 points, three songs in the season's top eight, one unanimous round win, and second place going to JB. The champion's margin was clean, and there was no asterisk — no matter how hard Grant lobbied for one.
