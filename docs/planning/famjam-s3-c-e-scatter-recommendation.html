1) Concept C — make it dead simple

Each player's season gets summarized as a 4-bucket taste profile. The buckets go from obvious to adventurous: Radio Hit → Recognizable → Curious Cut → Rabbit Hole. This is easy to read because it answers one simple question: what kind of songs does this person tend to submit?

What makes a pick a Radio Hit vs a Rabbit Hole?

Radio HitObscurity < 10. These are the most familiar, least discoverable picks in the sample.
RecognizableObscurity 10–19. Known songs, but not necessarily the most obvious possible pick.
Curious CutObscurity 20–29. A nice middle lane: discoverable without being ultra-obscure.
Rabbit HoleObscurity 30+. The kind of pick someone in the league is more likely to discover for the first time.
Why this works

It is much easier for players to grasp than a dense stat chart: you can immediately tell who lives in the Recognizable lane, who has a real Rabbit Hole habit, and who keeps drifting back toward Radio Hit territory.

3) Scatterplot — did the rabbit hole pay off?

This chart is the richer companion to Concept C. Each dot is one song submission. It shows whether a pick was adventurous and whether the league rewarded it. This is the best candidate for a richer desktop view because it tells a full story without replacing the simpler summary above.

How to read the plot

X-axis = discovery percentile. Left is more obvious / familiar; right is more rabbit-hole.

Y-axis = round vote percentile. Higher means the league rewarded the pick more.

Color = submitter. Point size = how out-of-character that pick was for that player compared with their usual obscurity range.

Why the size choice is useful

Because we do not yet have listener-breadth or playlist-play data, the bubble size uses a “surprise factor.” Bigger circles are songs that sit far away from that player's normal lane. That makes one-off outliers pop visually instead of quietly distorting the season story.
