## 1. Dave Jensen — "sounds too much like Jon (using big words). I don't think of him that way."

**VERDICT: confirmed, and the v1 dossier had it backwards.**

Measured across all 10 players (messages >= 40 chars):

| player | 9+ letter words | 12+ letter | formal connectives /1k | avg msg chars |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jon Black | 5.61% (1st) | 0.65% | 0.43 | 117 |
| Matt Mariani | 4.58% (7th) | 0.65% | 0.44 (3rd) | 86 |
| Dave Jensen | **3.88% (LAST)** | **0.49% (LAST)** | 0.12 (9th) | 83 |

Jensen is the plainest-vocabulary member of the group and Jon is the richest. Vocabulary
weight is one of the sharpest available contrasts BETWEEN them, and the v1 dossier
collapsed it. Any Jensen output using elevated diction is wrong.

**Action:** rewrite Jensen's §2 Voice and §6 Imitation kit to make plain, everyday
vocabulary an explicit positive rule, and add "never reaches for a formal or latinate
register — that is Jon's move, not his" to his §5 Negative space.

**Related:** Jensen's v1 profile already carries a section arguing he is hard for the
classifier to pin down. Worth re-examining whether that difficulty is partly an artifact
of the dossier describing the wrong person.

## 2. Matt Mariani — "I know my style is abrupt, but I think I also use big words occasionally"

**VERDICT: both halves confirmed — but the mechanism is register, not word length.**

- **Abrupt:** confirmed. 86-char average against Jon's 117 and Conor's 106.
- **Big words:** his raw long-word rate is mid-pack (7th of 10), so a length-based
  reading says no. But on formal connectives (*therefore, however, regarding,
  accordingly, nevertheless*) he ranks **3rd of 10** at 0.44/1k.

The accurate trait is therefore **"short messages that occasionally reach for a formal
register"** — not "uses big words". That is a sharper and much more imitable rule, and
the combination (clipped + occasional formality) is itself distinctive.

**Action:** revise §2 and §6 to state the register point explicitly, and make clear it
appears *occasionally* against a baseline of brevity — the contrast is the trait.
