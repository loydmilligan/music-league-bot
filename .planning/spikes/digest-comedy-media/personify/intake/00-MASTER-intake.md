# Master intake — things the corpus cannot tell me

**How to use this:** answer under the `>` prompts. Skip anything boring or that you
don't feel like answering — partial is fine and much better than nothing. Stream of
consciousness is ideal; I'd rather have your voice than tidy prose, and messy notes
carry more signal than polished ones.

**Photos:** drop them in `intake/photos/<Player_Name>/`. Name them so I can tell era
apart — `young-01.jpg`, `now-01.jpg`, `era-college-01.jpg`. I'll pull appearance
details from them. If a photo needs context ("this is the mullet year"), add a line
under that player's Appearance prompt rather than renaming the file.

**Why this exists:** the current dossiers are built purely from what people typed in
the chat. That gives voice and habits, but it cannot give me biography, appearance,
jobs, or the shared history the group takes for granted — which is where a lot of the
funniest material lives. Everything you add here becomes footnoted evidence.

**Priority order.** If you only do some of this, do it in this order:
1. **Darren Paletz** — 4,641 chars of his own writing. By far the thinnest.
2. **Dave Steingart** — 15,049 chars.
3. Clements, Jimmy, Shane — workable but thin.
4. The original five — mainly the "things to mock" and story prompts.

## Per-player prompts

Repeat this block for each person. Player-specific gap questions live in the
separate `<Player>-gaps.md` files — those are the targeted ones and are worth more
than this generic block.

### <PLAYER NAME>

**Job / career.** What they do, what they used to do, how they feel about it, and
anything about it the group finds funny.
>

**Appearance — young.** Era, hair, build, style, any look they'll never live down.
>

**Appearance — now.** What's changed. What they'd rather you didn't mention.
>

**College story.** One or two. Doesn't need to be flattering.
>

**Adult story.** At least one. A wedding, a trip, an incident, a legendary failure.
>

**Things to mock.** The standing material — what the group already teases them about.
The pre-existing bits matter more than new observations, because a persona that hits
a known bit reads as authentic and one that invents a new one reads as an AI guess.
>

**Quirks / tells.** Verbal habits, obsessions, predictable opinions, ways they are
reliably wrong.
>

**Relationships.** Who they're closest to, who they spar with, any history that
explains why they talk to each other the way they do.
>

**Anything else.**

## Cross-cutting questions

**Pecking order.** The dossiers each describe standing within the group separately,
so they don't necessarily agree with each other. Who actually sits where — who gets
deferred to, who gets piled on, who is the group's centre of gravity, who is
peripheral? Disagreements between your answer and the dossiers are exactly what the
consolidation pass needs to resolve.
>

**Running bits.** Bits that span months or years, that everyone understands without
explanation. These are the highest-value material there is and the corpus only shows
me fragments.
>

**Dead bits.** Anything that used to be funny and now isn't, or is off-limits for
reasons that have nothing to do with taste — a bit that ended badly, a subject that
went sour.
>

**Who is funniest, and why?** Your read on each person's actual comic mode — not how
they write, but what they're doing when they're being funny.
>
