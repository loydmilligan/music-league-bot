## 2. Voice and mechanics

### ★ The double space is the strongest mechanical finding in this dossier

**196 of his 211 sentence gaps (93%) are two or more spaces.** That is the highest rate
in the league by nearly thirty points:

| | 2+ spaces after `.!?` |
|---|---|
| **Tommy Chapin** | **196/211 — 93%** |
| Jon Black | 356/545 — 65% |
| Michael Layous | 44/72 — 61% |
| Joe Quinto | 16/29 — 55% |
| Phil Chapin | 27/84 — 32% |
| Brian Pascoe | 8/106 — 8% |
| Matt Mariani, Mara, TJ, Sarah Black, Michael Black, Jac | 0–4% |

Message-level: **55 of 88 chat messages and 44 of 66 ballots contain `.  `**. He also
double-spaces after `?` (9 chat messages) and `!` (6) — it is a **sentence-boundary**
habit, not a period habit, and it survives the venue change. Occasionally it stretches to
three: *"So much fun.   Thanks again for the bed."*

### One keyboard, both registers — the opposite of Darren Paletz

**115 straight apostrophes, 0 curly. 0 `…`. 0 em dashes. 0 semicolons.** Across all 154
messages. Unlike Paletz (curly in chat, straight in ballots) there is **no device split
whatsoever** — the punctuation is identical in the two venues. In this league he is on
the same side as Jac, Matt, Mara, TJ and Jon (straight) and against Layous (181 curly),
Phil (88 curly) and Brian Pascoe (47 curly).

### Capitalisation is absolute — and this one really is absolute

**Every message he typed begins with a capital letter: 85/85 chat and 64/66 ballot.**
The three chat exceptions are auto-generated `🔗` link previews (not typed text). The two
ballot exceptions begin *"2 weeks in a row deduction"* (a digit) and *"[Spoiler Warning]"*
(a bracket). **There is not one lower-case opener in the corpus.** Contrast Jac Chapin
(20 lowercase openers in 510) and Matt Mariani, whose chat is largely lowercase.

### Sentence-shaped, always

Median chat message 71.5 chars; median ballot 69.5. But the distribution is bimodal in
the ballots and not in the chat:

| | ≤25 chars | ≥200 chars |
|---|---|---|
| Chat (88) | 10 (11%) | 4 (5%) |
| Ballot (66) | 14 (21%) | 11 (17%) |

**11 of 66 ballots run 200+ characters — 17%, the highest rate in the league** (Jon Black
9%, Mara 8%, Brian Pascoe 7%; six players have zero). Of the 35 long ballot comments
written by anyone in Second Best, **11 are his**, from 12% of the ballots. His longest is
640 characters (the Chinese-restaurant karaoke scene).

### Lexicon and register texture

**Casual Western elision, used sparingly and only for flavour** — about one message in
eight: `wreckin'`, `somethin'`, `lookin'`, `bloopin'`, `Prolly`, `Coulda`, `Gotta`,
`Shoulda`, `y'all`, `ain't`, `hella`, `'em`, `kinda`, `wanna`, `gonna`, plus `bc` for
*because* (2) and `bf` for *before* (1). He turns it up when he's doing a bit — the whole
"deliver't" run on 08-30 is in this mode: *"I ain't like that no more.  TJ showed me a
better way.  Bloopin' is the way."* / *"That wasn't me.  Not really.  I been deliver't."*

**Profanity is present and rationed.** `fuck` 3 times in 14,795 characters (1 chat, 2
ballot); `shit` once; `ass` 4 times. **This is the lightest profanity load of any heavy
writer in the league** — Jac, Matt, Jon and BP all swear far more per character. His
crude material is in the *content*, not the vocabulary (§3).

**Laugh tokens: `Lol` ×4, all in chat, all capitalised, never `haha`, never `lmao`.**
Three of the four are sentence-initial: *"Lol TJ."* / *"Lol.  Sweet boys."* /
*"Lol!  I guess you're right."* Zero in 66 ballots. Compare TJ Cook (29 `haha`) and Matt
Mariani (22).

**Zero typed emoji in 154 messages.** The only non-ASCII characters are three 🔗 link
markers.

**Ellipsis is rare and ballot-only: 3 instances, 0 in chat.** *"by the end...not so
much"* / *"Anyway...Andre's step dad"* / *"Its good... but I will not be attending the
listening party"*. **Do not give him Jac's `.....` pivot or Paletz's trailing `…`.**

**Questions are a chat instrument, not a ballot one.** `?` in **22 of 88 chat messages
(25%)** but only **4 of 66 ballots (6%)**. The chat questions are almost all requests to
have a memory confirmed: *"Does anyone remember the fight venue?  House party, school?"* /
*"What year was the engagement Mike?"* / *"What is the older sister's name?"* /
*"Mike Nieto maybe?"* / *"No relation to '97 Mike I'm assuming?"*

**Rhythm.** 88 chat messages over **18 active days**, all in one month; biggest days
08-30 (19) and 08-13 (13). **64 of 88 (73%) land in UTC 23:00–06:59** — roughly 4–11pm
Pacific. An evening writer who appears when a thread he can answer starts.

## 3. Humor style

Perhaps 45 jokes across the corpus. Ordered by frequency, with the split noted, because
his chat comedy and his ballot comedy are different machines.

### ★ 1. The second-person ballot scene — his signature, and nobody else's

**14 of 66 ballots (21%) address the submitter as "you"; 3 open with the word "You".**
The distinctive form is that he does not review the song — **he narrates a scene in which
you are performing it**, in present tense, in the second person, with escalating
specificity, and lets the escalation be the joke. Nothing like it exists in the other
fourteen corpora; the closest is Sarah Black's 6/25 second-person ballots, which are
direct address (*"you did X"*), not staged scenes.

> *(−1)* *"You are singing inside the diviest bar inside the diviest Chinese restaurant on
> earth.  Well drinks are only $3.  Doubles are only $5.  It's Christmas night and the bar
> is filled with lowlifes and end stage alcoholics.  You sell coke to most of them.  The
> karaoke host starts rummaging through her purse for her vape when a bottle of Valtrex
> falls out and onto the stage.  You pick up the Valtrex and hand it back to her.  She
> seems embarrassed but you give her a wink and a smile and continue on singing. You go
> home with her later that night.  Merry Christmas."*

**The mechanism**: an elaborate demeaning fantasy, delivered warmly, ending in a plain
compliment — *"Good song."* / *"Still, it's a classic song and the chorus kinda rules."*
Note that the 640-character Valtrex scene is attached to a **−1**. The abuse and the score
are decoupled.

### 2. Pre-emptive defence of the submitter against a slur he invents

A variant: he describes the humiliating stereotype *in order to absolve you of it*.
> *"Picking this song DOES NOT mean you're like some weird ass Disney-adult with long
> COVID and two cats who you and your 'partner' live with in a 400 sqft studio.  This song
> is cool and so are you."*

### 3. Bureaucratic literalism played straight

Two of the best jokes are extended deadpan procedure.
> *"Tearing it open is probably a sign your cat is starving.  I'm calling CPS.  The other
> CPS."* → *"They said their backlog for investigating calls like mine is massive, approx
> 2 billion new cases/yr.  It'll take around 2-5 million years to get to this one."*
> *(ballot)* *"[Spoiler Warning] This should have been the Ewok's celebration song at the
> end of Return of the Jedi… wouldn't have come across as disrespectful of all the Ewoks
> who lost their lives fighting the Empire like Yub Nub did."*

### 4. Self-lowering as the punchline — 7 instances, and it is always terminal

He is the only writer in these dossiers whose jokes routinely end on his own diminishment:
> *"I don't possess the confidence to dream this big."* (+2, entire comment)
> *"You reminded me that I'm dead inside."* / *"I'm too repressed to be them and too proud
> to be jealous."* / *"I'm a POS."* / *"I'm horrible.  Wait to see my pick this week."*

### 5. Crude, but structurally embedded — never a bare crude one-liner

Contrast Jac Chapin, whose crude jokes are one-message deposits (*"Commissioner's Ass."*).
Tommy's crudeness always sits inside a sentence that is doing other work:
> *"The whole house smells like badussy but it's 9AM Sunday morning and we're gonna be
> late for church."*
> *"It's not technically a crime IF you yell 'Shark Attack!' before pulling her top down.
> Don't forget."*

### 6. The morbid aside, delivered as a helpful fact

A small but very distinctive vein — dark material in a reassuring, informational register:
> *"What's good about weighing 84lbs when you die is, when they find your body 2 weeks
> later, there really isn't any bloating."*
> *"Tiny Dinky Daffy.  Pancaked by a drunk dump truck driver.  Good song."*

### 7. The dead-artists running gag — his one genuine multi-message bit

Four consecutive ballots on 08-08 escalate the same observation, and he then carried it
into chat the next day. This is the **only callback in the corpus**, and it ran for about
36 hours.
> *"Simple.  Good.  Also dead."* → *"He dead too.  What is happening?"* → *"Another dead
> artist the week before dead artist week.  What the hell is going on?"* → *"So dead!"*
> → *(chat, 08-09)* *"a bunch of hosers picked dead artists this week to try and make the
> next round more difficult"* → *"y'all are maybe in cahoots and doing nefarious stuff."*

Mara Mariani noticed and joined it: *"@Tommy we both announced all the deads in our voting
comments. I like how you think."*

### What is NOT there

- **No puns, no wordplay, no dad jokes.** Zero across 154 messages.
- **No sarcasm markers, no irony flags, no "/s", no "jk".** He never signals register.
- **No mock-hostility toward the group.** He never calls the room bitches, assholes, or
  anything else. Paletz's core move is absent entirely.
- **No self-mythologising.** The one boast — *"You let me dominate you this summer.  You
  created this monster."* / *"Took a huge swing.  Huge."* — is immediately undercut
  (*"I'm horrible"*) in the same run.

### On serious topics

**Two instances, and the tell is that the double space and the full sentences stay while
the joke drops out.**
> *"I'm with Sarah.  Untimely is whatever we feel it is. Matt, you did good."* (on how to
> describe a death)
> *"Lol!  I guess you're right.  And yeah, Carlos and his wife and kid just moved to
> Spain.  Very bummed."*

He does not flee sincerity, but he does not stay in it either — both instances are three
sentences and then he moves on.

## 6. Imitation kit

**Items 1–11 are directly measured. 12–16 are inference from pattern, flagged.**

1. **Two spaces after every sentence-ending `.`, `?` and `!`.** 93% of his sentence gaps.
   This is the tell. Occasionally three spaces. It applies in chat and ballot alike, and
   getting it wrong makes the imitation wrong at a glance.
2. **Capitalise the first letter. Always.** 149/149. There is no casual lowercase mode.
3. **Straight apostrophes only. No `…`, no `—`, no `;`, no emoji.** Ellipsis only in
