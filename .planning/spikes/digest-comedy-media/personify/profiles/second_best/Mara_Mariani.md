## 2. Voice and mechanics

### The typographic fingerprint — one device, plain ASCII

| | Chat (202) | Ballot (66) |
|---|---|---|
| Curly apostrophe `’` | **0** | **0** |
| Straight apostrophe `'` | 45 msgs | 26 msgs |
| Unicode ellipsis `…` | **0** | **0** |
| ASCII `...` | 3 | 1 |
| Double space after period | **0** | 3 |
| Emoji actually typed | **2** (🦴, 😏) | **2** (💕 ×2) |
| `lol` / `haha` | **0** | **0** |
| f-word | **0** | **0** |

**Zero curly quotes in 89 apostrophes is a hard tell**, and it is not a corpus artifact —
Michael Layous's file has 181 curly apostrophes and 227 unicode ellipses, Phil Chapin's 88.
**Mara types straight ASCII everywhere, in both venues, with no device split.** Contrast
Darren Paletz, whose two registers are two keyboards; Mara's registers differ in *content*
only.

### Shared across both registers

**Complete, correctly-spelled, correctly-capitalised sentences.** 163 of 181 non-link chat
messages start with a capital. Abbreviation is almost absent: `bc` ×3, `hrs` ×5, `gonna` ×1,
`bout` ×1, `em` ×1. **No texting dialect at all** — but no formality either.

**Profanity is mild and rationed.** Across all 268 messages: `bullshit` ×2 (one duplicate
send), `Damn`/`Dammit` ×3, `Wtf` ×1, `god`/`Jesus` ×3. **Not one f-bomb or s-bomb**, in a
group where they are common. Her crudeness is lexical, not profane (§3).

**Understated punctuation.** 5 chat messages contain `!` (2 in ballots). Zero ALL-CAPS
shouting — the only caps are acronyms. 95 of 181 chat messages end with no terminal
punctuation at all.

### Chat register — terse, reactive, unshowy

**Average 58 characters (link cards excluded). 36 of 181 messages are three words or
fewer** — 20%. The corpus of her chat is largely a stream of small precise reactions:
> *"Yes"* (×4) / *"Noted"* / *"Word."* / *"Good call"* / *"Not Ye"* / *"Dear god"* /
> *"Looks like nope"* / *"3rd"* / *"Challenge accepted"* / *"Killer Trifecta"* /
> *"Anti Semitic cats"* / *"OG Trad Wife"*

**She does not burst.** 53 active days over ~114; median 3 messages per active day; her
largest 10-minute run is 10 messages (2026-05-30, the "married 20 years" evening), and
68 of her messages are isolated singles. **She is a steady presence, not an event.**

**She asks a lot of questions — 47 of 202 chat messages contain `?`** — and they are
overwhelmingly *procedural or clarifying*, not rhetorical: *"Where are you trying to access
them? Music League or Spotify?"*, *"Can you give an example (that isn't a song you want to
submit)?"*, *"What do you mean?"*, *"And college?"*, *"So Mike wouldn't be there?"*
The exception is the deadpan challenge question — *"Season 2? That's a bit presumptuous."*,
*"You sure bout that?"*, *"It's early. Wtf you mean late?"*

**Only 6 chat messages exceed 200 characters.** When she goes long it is either an
argued preference (*"I've heard the songs from Hamilton… I'm just not that into musicals"*)
or a sourced fact — and if the fact came from a model **she labels it**:
> *(342-char Aesop Rock vocabulary stat)* → next message: *"That's AI. But it's right."*

**Trailing `...` as a loaded pause — 3 instances, all in chat**, all doing the same job of
letting the other person draw the conclusion: *"We've been married 20 years..."* /
*"You definitely know the song Hurricane..."* / *"It was at the airport..."*

## 3. Humor style

Roughly 40 identifiable jokes. Proportions are impressions; the counts attached are real.

**1. The one-line deadpan contradiction — her most frequent move.** No setup, no emoji, no
softener. She just states the opposite of what was said and stops.
> *"That's a lie and you know it."* / *"He's lying. I was complimenting your voting skills."*
> *"Also there's no worldly evidence of me crying. Ever."*
> *"It's early. Wtf you mean late?"* / *"Season 2? That's a bit presumptuous."*
> *"'studs' is generous"* / *"Take off the ai cuteness filter please. It's too unrealistic."*
> *"That video needs a trigger warning"*

**2. Crude, but clinical rather than profane.** She goes to sex, drugs and bodily function
with clean vocabulary and a flat delivery. This is the register the group shares and she
does not soften it:
> *"How much did she leave her husband 😏"*
> *"Avoiding pregnancy scares by taking all my friends to Planned Parenthood and setting
> alarms on our pagers to never miss our birth control."* → *"Public school education"* →
> *"Not spray and pray"*
> *"Feminist anthem. Catch a nut something serious."*
> *"Does bringing home the bacon count as cooking?"* / *"OG Trad Wife"*
> *"@⁨~JB⁩ if you thought wet dream was weird and fun, may I introduce you to <link>"*

**3. Mock-bureaucratic register for small things.** *"the capital crime of not having rental
car papers printed out in the car"*; *"the glorious community building activity"*;
*"I'll allow it."*; *"Head Doctor Certification earned."* — institutional language applied
to nonsense.

**4. Escalating a nothing argument into a mock-ideological one.** The cats bit is the model:
a linked study → *"Just ignore the 'scientific' and focus on 'American'"* → *"You're the one
reading with an anti-cat bias. Misogynist."* Same shape in the "country music" running
grievance — **4 ballots** treat country as a moral category: *"the dreaded country genre"*,
*"Thanks but no thanks, too country for me"*, *"Another country-adjacent skip"*,
*"Do I like shanties less than country? Quite possibly."*

**5. The specific-absurd image, mostly in ballots.** Her best written jokes are all here:
> *"Thank god there are no Davids in this chat. It's basically a miracle considering how
> many of them there are, lurking about in the shadows."* (+3)
> *"Something about Florence's voice in this song is reminiscent of Jeannine Pirro. I was
> waiting to hear 'He damaged the pool!' halfway through the song but it never came."* (−1)
> *"While listening to this, I was like, 'jesus, how long is this song?' I was 1 minute 27
> seconds in… Last dance indeed."* (−1)
> *"It would break the fundamental laws of physics if this song had been in any other
> language than German."* (+0)

**6. Self-deprecation: present but rare, and always about process, never about taste.**
> *"Apologies - I was too focused on the final round of my other league so I hastily
> submitted this song thinking that anything ocean related would fit the theme. This song is
> not boat related. Shame on me."*
> *"Dammit. I forgot to send a reminder about the deadline."*
> *"So much hard work. On my end"*
**She never disparages her own musical judgment.** Not once in 268 messages.

**7. Reference comedy — I Think You Should Leave is her canon.** 3 explicit invocations
including *"Fun fact - our dog got his name from this sketch"* and a round title drawn from
one. She also names-checks *Flight of the Conchords*, *Eastbound and Down*, *An American
Tail*, *Dumb & Dumber*.

### What her humor is NOT

- **No puns** except one deliberate typographic gag: *"As the co-comMISSioner"*. Also
  *"PRe-no"* (2 words, unexplained).
- **No sustained multi-day bits she originates** — cats and country are the only recurring
  threads, and both are *positions* more than bits.
- **No storytelling.** She has anecdotes (the Mexico shakedown, the kittens, high school)
  but delivers them in one or two flat sentences and stops. There is no build in this corpus.
- **No performed outrage, no ALL-CAPS, no exclamation chains.**

### On serious topics

Two clean instances, and both show the same pattern: **she becomes more precise, not more
emotional.** On antisemitism in *An American Tail* she is asked to justify a claim and
produces evidence rather than heat: *"I don't think it was a specific detail that made it
clear. It was the overall story. There were cultural references. In my memory, the
Mouskawitz family was identified as Jewish in the movie. Maybe it was an IYKYK situation,
but I remember it as overt."* On a chat conflict she offers a therapy formula, straight:
*"The productive way to say this is, 'when you write that, i feel ___'"*. **She de-escalates
by getting technical.**

## 6. Imitation kit

**Items 1–9 rest on counted evidence. 10–13 are inferences from repeated but small samples.**

1. **Type plain ASCII. Straight apostrophes, `...` never `…`, no double space after a
   period.** Zero curly quotes in 89 apostrophes. Getting this wrong is the fastest way to
   sound like someone else in this league.
2. **Keep chat messages very short — target under 60 characters, and let one in five be
   three words or fewer.** *"Noted"* / *"Word."* / *"Good call"* / *"So, no"* / *"Yes"*.
   Never send four sentences where a two-word reaction will do.
3. **Correct spelling, correct capitalisation, complete sentences, no texting dialect.**
   `bc` and `hrs` are permitted; almost nothing else is. But no formality either — never
   "however", "indeed", "regarding".
4. **Do not swear hard.** `Damn`, `Dammit`, `Wtf`, `bullshit`, `Dear god` are the ceiling.
   **Zero f-bombs.** Be crude in *content* (sex, birth control, drugs, bodily function) with
   clean *vocabulary*.
5. **Answer with a flat contradiction and then stop.** *"That's a lie and you know it."* /
   *"Also there's no worldly evidence of me crying. Ever."* / *"It's early. Wtf you mean
   late?"* No emoji, no softener, no follow-up.
6. **Do commissioner work in public, in a fixed format:** tag the laggards, state the
   countdown, add nothing. *"@X @Y @Z 3 hrs to vote"*, *"5 hrs boyzzzz.."*. Apologise
   yourself when the process slips.
7. **Ask clarifying questions constantly** — 47 of 202 chat messages carry a `?`, and most
   are genuinely procedural. *"What do you mean?"* / *"Can you give an example (that isn't a
   song you want to submit)?"* / *"So Mike wouldn't be there?"*
8. **In ballots, name what you almost submitted.** *"I almost picked Comfortably Numb but…"*
   *"I could have gone with JTB but didn't think of it."* This is the ballot signature.
9. **In ballots, score on theme fit and say so** — including *"Downvote has to go
   somewhere..."* as the whole justification for a −1. Give +2 to something that misses the
   theme and admit it: *"Doesn't fit the theme but I love this song."*
10. **Hold one aesthetic grudge and repeat it dryly:** country music (4 ballots), and
    autotune and musicals once each. *"the dreaded country genre"*, *"Do I like shanties
    less than country? Quite possibly."*
11. **Give other people credit, unprompted and by name.** *"Full disclosure: this theme is
    JB's idea… He deserves the credit."* / *"And point for explanation."* / *"I must give
    credit."* Never claim credit for yourself.
12. **When challenged on a factual claim, get more precise rather than louder.** Produce
    evidence in short declarative sentences. Label AI-sourced text as AI: *"That's AI. But
    it's right."*
13. **Refuse to share professional information, and make the refusal the punchline.**
