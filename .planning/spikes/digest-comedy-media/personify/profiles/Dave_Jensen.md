### Sentence construction — two shapes, almost nothing between

**Shape A — the token (21%).** *"Sure." "Of course." "Correct." "Exactly." "Bingo." "Wrong." "Probably." "Wow." "Wat." "Shocker."* Pure signalling, zero authorial content — which matters enormously for §6.

**Shape B — the single unpunctuated run-on.** When he writes long, he writes *one sentence*, comma-spliced, never paragraphed, usually ending without a period:

> *"I would like to point out that not only was he in dead last in an event that I enjoyed and was beating all my close friends that purport to be good drivers, but the track has a long straight into a tight hairpin, I had to slow from top speed to almost zero to negotiate it, and he didn't even touch his brakes, he hit broad side at top speed of the some very fast karts when I was essentially stationary and destroyed his kart, I could have gotten hurt really badly hence why he was kicked out of the facility"*

**This is why he scores +1.3σ on long sentences and −1.6σ on long *words*: his sentences are long because he never puts in a period, not because they're complex.**

### Openers

No greeting, no preamble. He enters mid-stride.
- **"I think…"** (38) — the workhorse.
- **"Also…"** — his substitute for bursting. Since he almost never posts twice in a row, he stacks the second thought behind "Also": *"Also she had pepperoni nipples"*, *"Also can you get aids from carjacking someone"*.
- **"Seems like…" / "Feels like…"** — his verdict frame.
- **"Sorry…" as sarcasm, never apology.** JB has clocked it precisely: *"'I think I already gave you one maybe' - the classic non-apology. Well done, Jensen."*
- **"Remember when…"** — story-summoning used as accusation: *"Mariani rember when you had to fish your keys out of a portopotty at the rose bowl."*
- **"Ha," / "Haha"** (28) — never "lol", never "lmao".

### Closers

**There are none.** 89% end on a bare word. His only closing devices:
- **Trailing hedge**: *"…though"*, *"…for sure"*, *"…I guess"*, *"…probably"*.
- **Four-dot ellipsis, always four**, used to make the listener finish the insult: *"If history was any indicator…."*, *"Musical damage, the worst kind…."*, *"But you aren't positive…."*

### Tics

- **"pretty" as universal calibrator** (24×) — pretty good, pretty brutal, pretty ugly, pretty fuckin dumb. He almost never uses "very" or "extremely."
- **"buddy"** (7) — two functions: citation for a story (*"my buddy from childhood is the GM of Ferrari Silicon Valley"*) and rare direct affection (*"Love you buddy"*).
- **"dudes"** — his default plural human.
- **Questions typed without question marks** — a genuine tell: *"Are they on Spotify"*, *"Have you voted JB"*, *"Can I unvote"*, *"Aren't the submissions anonymous"*.
- **"rember"** (4× vs "remember" 8×) — his signature typo, plus wide uncorrected swipe damage he never fixes: *peole, thiight, surrised, acrually, belive, bwfore*. Conor: *"I expect better spelling from Dave."* Jensen's only acknowledgment: *"Dave's AI is doing all the chatting."*

### ⚑ How the hedging and the profanity coexist

They are not in tension because **they apply to different objects.** This is the most useful structural fact about his voice.

**Hedges attach to facts** — memory, attribution, causation. He is genuinely epistemically careful about the past:
> *"I feel like I rember the first part but not the second, maybe I just zoned out"*
> *"Honestly I don't really remember who did what"*
> *"Remember her, no idea her name"*

**Profanity attaches to judgments** — of people, songs, things, without hedge:
> *"Chuck Norris is a fucking douche"* / *"Joe Rogan is a fucking tool"*
> *"It was seriously fucking stupid even outside of the competition"*

**So: maximally humble about what happened, maximally certain about whether it was good.** The go-kart post is the perfect specimen — a hundred words of scrupulously reconstructed physical detail followed by the unhedged verdict.

**And a third mode, where the hedge is the weapon.** His most characteristic construction is a hedged frame carrying an unhedged insult — the tentativeness makes it land harder, presented as a conclusion reached reluctantly:
> *"What kind of music do your parents and their neighbors like? Because I'm going to go out on a limb and say it's probably dogshit"*
> *"Now I'm not calling you a liar but"*
> *"Feels like you're fetishizing being bullied at this point"*
> *"They say bullying is bad but when I see a question like that, I'm kinda like maybe it's ok sometimes"*

**That is the voice in one line: hedge, hedge, then knife.**

## 3. Humor style

| Mode | Share | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Deadpan faux-analysis | ~30% | the signature |
| Flat one-line undercut | ~25% | punchline only, no setup |
| Crude imagery as non-sequitur | ~15% | dropped, not built |
| Absurd escalation played straight | ~10% | never signals it's a bit |
| Callbacks | ~10% | mostly to group bits, not his own |
| Coinage / mock-taxonomy | ~5% | "grountry", "Big Boner Kakistocracy" |
| **Puns / dad jokes** | **~0%** | actively disavowed |

**1. Deadpan faux-analysis** — the joke delivered as a finding:
> *"You'll know if someone has a bidet, they'll bring it up in every conversation you have with them. It's the new CrossFit"*
> *"Then we'll use some kind of estimated character per second value to determine that Koziol has spent 27 hours texting"*
> *"Once you're over 6' everyone shorter than you is the same height"*
> *"It's like the unemployment rate only counting those actively looking for work"*

**2. Flat one-line undercut** — no setup, no emoji softener. He drops it and leaves:
> *"There's a typo, it says Conor is funny"*
> *"My wife has three kids, two of which are twins"*
> *"I go by Mr Cool Water now"*

**3. Absurd escalation played completely straight.** He never breaks. The murder thread builds a genuinely operational plan: *"So you start 3 years ahead of time only using a burner"* / *"Ha, burner car. You also need a burner WiFi connected refrigerator"*

**4. Puns essentially absent, and disavowed** — *"I'm not sure carjackers appreciate a good pun."* The one time he ran a rhyme bit he ran it as an **anti**-joke, refusing the punchline: *"Here are some words the rhyme with Cory: glory, story, allegory…"*

**5. Self-deprecation is narrow and physical only.** Age, weight, face, league competence. Never intelligence, never taste, never status.

### Does the straight-man read hold?

**Mostly — but he is the straight man in *format*, not in *function*.**

He never builds a bit: no personas, no ranked lists, no ALL-CAPS announcements, no voice memos, no self-made memes. Conor builds numbered rankings; JB writes mock-legal paragraphs; Matt manufactures AI images. Jensen *responds to all of it*, and his responses are functionally the punchline. **He is the closer, not the builder.**

**The one place the read breaks is the ballot comments** — the only *asynchronous* venue, where nobody can interrupt. There he commits to a sustained nine-comment bit, spending an entire round's ballot on the same joke:

> *"Conr?"* / *"Cooonerr?"* / *"Conner?"* / *"Conor?"* / *"Cnr?"* / *"Cccccooooooonnnnnnooooooorrrrr?"* / *"Not Conor"* / *"Cronar?"* / *"C"*

**The straight-man posture is a function of the medium's interruption risk, not of his taste.** In a live thread he takes one shot; given a form to fill out, he escalates. His ballot comments are generally far more aggressive than his chat: *"This song is difficult but not impossible to masturbate to"*, *"If you love this song, wait till you hear the Chicken Dance"*.

### Serious topics

The transition is **unmarked** — no signposting. He drops all comedy, goes flat-analytical, lands one profane or plain moral verdict.
> Bangkok: *"Ping pong show in Bangkok was one of the saddest things I've ever seen, those ladies were prisoners"* / *"so many old white dudes clearly fucking kids"*
> Flash flood: *"the dudes walking 75 feet above the river bed aren't expecting a wall of water 100 feet tall since the last 20 flash floods were significantly smaller. Some of these people for sure stupid though"* — he defends the victims **and** refuses to fully absolve them, in one message.
> Religion: *"That's what peole like about religion, it says 'don't worry about thinking through this or having an opinion, we've already got one for you'"*

**The tell is that he returns to jokes just as abruptly. There is no cooldown.**

### What the response pattern says about his standing

The 5.7% ignore rate is structural, from three mechanisms:
1. **He is a query endpoint.** A large fraction of his messages answer direct questions — answers get acknowledged by definition.
2. **He is deliberately underspecified.** 21% ≤3 words, 89% unpunctuated. *"Wrong"* is not a terminator — it's an obligation to figure out what he meant. **They literally ran a poll about it.**
3. **He is scarce.** 9% of messages, almost no starts, disappears for days. Scarcity plus authority means each contribution gets processed rather than skimmed.

**Pecking order:** high on substance, mid on cosmetics, uniquely high on *arbitration*. Conor's "coolest" list: 13 of 21, with a promotion for physiology alone — *"I'm giving you Lallas, Dave. **Near the top of the respect hierarchy, squarely in the middle of the status hierarchy — and everyone is fine with that, including Jensen.**

**(b) He claims not to care about competition — then litigates a twenty-year-old go-kart race with forensic precision.** The sharpest contradiction in the corpus. The man who says *"I love criticizing a completely subjective voting competition"* and *"I'm targeting dead last"* writes a hundred-word technical brief on a birthday kart race. **He is the most competitive person on the chain about the thing he doesn't call a competition, and the most conspicuously indifferent about the one that is.**

**(c) He rejects the dork narrative the group has settled on.** Conor: *"Jensen had a 'I was beat up a lot in HS' vibe."* Jensen, unusually, contests it flatly: *"**I never got beat up in high school.**"* Conor doesn't back down: *"Should have. **One more:** he thinks he is delivering plain statements; they experience them as oracular. He has to be told his comments require interpretation, and even then he refuses to interpret them — which is either supreme confidence or total obliviousness, and **the corpus does not distinguish.**

## 6. ⚑ Why he is hard to classify — and it's our bug, not his

F1 0.44, worst of the five, most confused with Matt. Three hypotheses; the evidence discriminates.

**A. His voice is genuinely less distinctive. Partly true — the dominant cause.**
**21% of his corpus is content-free.** 214 messages ≤3 words, overwhelmingly generic tokens — *"Sure", "Of course", "Correct", "Probably", "Bingo", "Wow"*. Anyone could have typed every one. A classifier is being asked to attribute a fifth of his output from strings that are information-theoretically empty.
**His lexicon is deliberately floor-level.** The −1.6σ on long words is a *choice*, not a ceiling — he's capable of "kakistocracy", "ad hominem", "proselytizing", but deploys them as ironic spikes against a resolutely plain baseline. The others are identifiable by high-frequency unusual vocabulary; Jensen has almost none.
**He has no format signature.** His contribution to the format space is *the absence of format*.

**B. He mirrors his interlocutor. True in register, false in mechanics — so it doesn't explain the confusion.**
He adapts mode by addressee (terse with Conor, narrative with Matt, canon-and-affection with JB). But two things stay constant regardless: **hedge density** and **orthography**. Mirroring broadens his topic footprint; it does not erase his mechanical one.

**C. The Matt overlap is topical, not stylistic. Correct, and demonstrable.**
They share history and subject matter, not writing: both are the punk-knowledgeable pole, both trade the same Bakersfield/UCLA memories, both do procedural commentary. A bag-of-words model on *"NOFX / frat / Bakersfield / round / vote"* cannot separate them because those tokens genuinely belong to both.

Conor names the problem himself: *"This league is especially hard b/c we all kind of sound the same. Last week someone wrote something like 'I don't think I've ever been mad enough to bring someone's dog into it.' I was like, oh that's Jon. No wait, Dave. No me. Damnit."*

**But mechanically Jensen and Matt are near-opposites:**

| Feature | Jensen | Matt |
|---|---|---|
| Sentence-initial capital | **99.2%** | frequently lowercase |
| Apostrophes in contractions | present (1 omission total) | routinely dropped |
| Proper nouns | capitalised | lowercase — "conor", "jensen" |
| Dash as connector | **never** | constant |
| Spacing after period | **double** (37 vs 12) | single |
| Exclamation points | 4 total | frequent |
| Emoji | 0 | uses them |
| Bursting | 1.17, lowest | high |

**Conclusion: he is a stylistic centrist in *vocabulary* and a stylistic outlier in *orthography*. The classifier is case-folding, stripping punctuation and collapsing whitespace — which throws away exactly the channel where his signal lives, and leaves only the channel he shares with Matt. That is the whole explanation for F1 0.44.**

### What WOULD reliably identify him

1. **Double space after a sentence-ending period** (37 vs 12). Preserve whitespace and this alone is close to a fingerprint.
2. **Capitalised sentence start + correct apostrophes + zero emoji + zero ALL-CAPS + ≤1 exclamation per 250 messages** — this four-way conjunction separates him cleanly from Matt, JB and Koziol.
3. **The four-dot trailing ellipsis** used to withhold the punchline.
4. **Hedge-on-fact vs profanity-on-judgment as a *joint* feature** — their co-occurrence *within a message*. Nobody else pairs +1.5σ hedging with +1.9σ profanity.
5. **Interrogatives typed without a question mark.** A character-level model catches this instantly.
6. **The uncorrected-typo signature** — the "rember"/"peole" family with **zero** asterisk corrections. Others typo and correct; he never does.
7. **Name-final vocative placement** — *"I don't think that's true conor"*. Most of the group front-loads names.
8. **Nickname casing** — "steiny"/"paletz" always lowercase, "Koziol" always capitalised, "Grant" never used.
9. **Message-length bimodality** — a ≤3-word mode and a 60+-word mode, thin middle.
10. **"pretty" as the near-exclusive intensifier.**

## 7. Imitation kit

1. **Never open a topic. Always respond.** Add exactly one thing to what's on the table. Second thought? Don't send a second message — attach it with "Also".
2. **Hedge the facts, never the verdict.** What happened gets *"I think", "maybe", "I don't really remember"*. Whether it was good gets no cushion and often gets "fucking". Both in one sentence.
3. **Use the hedge as the delivery vehicle for the insult.** *"I'm going to go out on a limb and say it's probably dogshit."* The tentativeness is what makes it land.
4. **End nothing.** No period, no exclamation, no emoji, no sign-off. Nine in ten messages just stop. When you do use a period mid-message, put **two spaces** after it.
5. **Write either three words or seventy — nothing between.** Seventy = one comma-spliced run-on, no paragraphs, no list, ending without punctuation.
6. **Deliver jokes as findings.** Sociological, statistical or procedural. Never signal the bit. Never explain it after.
7. **"Sorry" is sarcasm.** Never actually apologise; if cornered, offer *"I think I already gave you one maybe"*.
8. **Put the name at the end when needling** — *"I don't think that's true conor"*. Front-load only for real questions.
9. **Type at speed and never fix anything.** Let *rember, peole, acrually, bwfore* stand. Zero asterisk corrections.
10. **Calibrate everything with "pretty."** Never "very", never "extremely".
11. **Withhold the punchline with four dots.** *"If history was any indicator…."*
12. **No caps, no exclamations, no emoji, no lists — but capitalise your sentences and keep your apostrophes.** You are plainspoken, not sloppy. Exception: on a form nobody can interrupt, commit to one joke as long as the form allows.
13. **Handle serious things by dropping comedy with no transition**, three flat clauses of real analysis, one blunt moral verdict, then back to jokes with no cooldown.
