# Educational Film — "How Rules Are Made"

> 16mm-classroom register. **Narration is written**; the two quoted ballots are verbatim.

```
[PROJECTOR RATTLE. TITLE CARD: "HOW RULES ARE MADE"]

NARRATOR:
    Second Best is a music league of fourteen people.
    It has ten written rules.

    [BEAT]

    On the second of August, an eleventh appeared.
    It was six words long and it was attached to a downvote.

    [VERBATIM]  "Ska rule -1, sorry"

NARRATOR:
    It was never proposed. It was never agreed to.
    It was applied once, to one song, by one man.

    [BEAT]

    Twenty-one days later it changed the behaviour of a player
    who had never voted on the original song at all.

    [VERBATIM]  "Will Mariani view this as too Ska"

NARRATOR:
    By then the rule had been abandoned by its author,
    who was in the middle of giving that very song three points.

    [BEAT]

    This is how law works in a group chat.
    Thank you for your attention.

[PROJECTOR STOPS]
```

## Why this format fits
The documentary register earns its keep only when it can point at a real mechanism, and
here there is one: a norm propagating past the person who invented it. That is a genuine
observation about the league, not a joke about the league, and the format is the only one
in the set that can hold an observation without deflating it.

The last narration line is the single best-written line in the spike, and it is also the
one that most clearly comes from the AI rather than the league — worth noting as a
boundary case for the "league supplies the joke" principle.

## Reservations
27 seconds and slow. The classroom-film voice is a well-worn AI-comedy default and would
read as a costume by the third use. Strong *one-off*; weak franchise. Compare
`official_correction`, which is the same institutional instinct in a container that
survives repetition.
