# Dramatic Reading — "Mash What Is The Ska Rule"

> The performer reads **one real chat message, complete and unaltered**, then the
> follow-up sent four minutes later. Not one word is added. The comedy is entirely in
> pacing and in what the message turns out to be about.

## Performance direction

```yaml
performer_persona: "not an impression of Sarah Zucker — a neutral narrator who has
  found the message in an archive and is reading it into a record"
pace: "brisk and businesslike through the middle; the last two words land alone"
tone: "someone delivering a research summary that they do not realise is a eulogy"
avoid:
  - "doing a voice"
  - "sarcasm on any line — the text is not sarcastic"
  - "laughing at the end"
```

## Script

```
[TAPE HISS. ONE MIC.]

READER [VERBATIM, chat, 2026-08-02T04:49:47Z]:

    "Mash what is the ska rule!? Automatic downvote?
     Loved everyone's comments. JB you're so right: My Town is likable but so
     simple and safe. I came across a really terrible song called San Diego by
     Bruce Springsteen about a drug mule and there is Blink 182's San Diego song
     but it wasn't a great candidate. And Crazy Poway Kids by Unwritten Law.
     For some reason I became fixated on representing my hometown.
     My runner ups were No Use For A Name's Fairytale of New York
     or Frank Sinatra's Chicago."

[FOUR SECONDS OF NOTHING]

CARD:  four minutes later

READER [VERBATIM, 2026-08-02T04:53:18Z]:

    "Dang I didn't know how intensely you disliked ska!

     Noted."

[STOP]
```

## Why this format fits
The message is a genuine artefact: someone doing real homework, showing their work,
being cheerful about it, then quietly closing the subject. Read straight it is funny and
slightly sad, and — crucially — it is funnier *if you already know* that the band she
names in the middle wins Mashew a round two weeks later. The format lets the audience
find that themselves, which is better than any bit that points at it.

## Reservations
Rated **specificity 2, not 3**, on purpose: taken alone this is a player being
characteristic, not a callback. It only reaches deep lore in context with the receipts
bit — which is an argument for ordering, not for cutting it.

Real risk: reading a real person's real message for laughs is the most exposed thing in
this spike. It survives here because she comes off well and Matt is the butt. A version
where the reader "does a voice" would be cruel and must never be built. The brief's
voice-cloning idea should be considered dead on this evidence — the neutral-archivist
read is better comedy *and* has none of the risk.
