# Breaking News

> Bracketed lines are performance direction. Quoted player text is **verbatim from the
> database** and is marked. Nothing here is invented dialogue.

```
[URGENT STING. LOWER THIRD: "SKA RULE — NO LONGER IN EFFECT"]

ANCHOR:
The Ska Rule is not being enforced.

[BEAT]

Introduced August second by a single voter, applied once, never written down —
on August twenty-third its author gave a NOFX song three points.
His stated reason, in full:

[ON SCREEN, VERBATIM VOTE COMMENT — "Always love this album more than others"]

ANCHOR:
Voting on the same song, Michael Layous wrote — and we are quoting the ballot —
"Will Mariani view this as too Ska."

[BEAT]

ANCHOR:
Mr. Layous was complying with a rule that had already been abandoned.
Nobody has told him.
```

## Why this format fits
Broadcast urgency is the cheapest possible engine for a joke whose whole premise is that
something small was treated as important. The bit works because the "developing story" is
a genre rule invented by one man in a comment box.

## Reservations
This is the weakest of the strong candidates. It uses two beats and the anchor has to
*explain* the situation before the punchline can land — which trips Quality Rule 5 (avoid
explaining the joke) and Rule 8 (avoid excessive setup). The format wants a single event;
this incident is a chain. It also has the highest slop risk of anything here: "breaking
news voice" is the most-imitated register in AI comedy and would be the first thing to
feel generic on a fifth use.
