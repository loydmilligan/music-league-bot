# Music League bits — what else we could make

Companion to the courtroom video. This is the menu.

**The idea in one line:** the bot already reads every ballot, every comment and every
message in the group chat, so it can find the thing nobody noticed — the contradiction,
the old quote, the vote that decided something — and hand it back as a short bit.

**The rule we're working to:** the league supplies the joke, the AI supplies the format.
A generic roast is worthless. A twenty-second bit that quotes something you actually wrote
three weeks ago is not.

Everything quoted below is **real** — pulled from the database, not written by the AI. That
constraint is doing most of the work.

## Made

### ⚖️ The Court of Musical Appeals — *the video you just watched*
Cross-examination. Counsel produces the defendant's own words, the defendant wilts, the
judge convicts. Ends on the defendant's own chat message rather than on a verdict, which is
both funnier and the only thing that keeps it fair.

**Source:** Boarz R145. Conor downvotes Jimmy's Tequila — *"The rest of us put thought into
this, effort… To flaunt your ignorance?"* — and on the same ballot submits a song he can't
identify (*"I have no idea to this day what those 2 Italian ladies were signing about"*) in
a round themed **lyrics not in English**. He finishes last, two below Jimmy. Then, in chat:
*"I went pretty hard after Tequila. Then it beat me. Soooo…I should keep my lard ass mouth
closed."*

**~60s. Best format we have.** Works because there's a villain, a victim, and he convicts
himself.

### 📺 Attack Ad
Political hit piece. Same hypocrisy, thirty seconds, no courtroom. Ends on a disclaimer
that's a real fact — *"Not paid for by Jimmy Troy, who gave Conor's song a point anyway."*

**~40s. Second best.** Cheapest to make and the most repeatable.

### 🏛️ Official Correction
The Office of the Commissioner issues a ruling nobody asked for. Built for R145's
*"is tequila an English word"* thread — which the league argued for days and **never
resolved**. The joke is that the ruling arrives weeks late, settles it, and changes
nothing: the song already finished on zero.

The best part is real — the guy who submitted it argued the side that *disqualified his own
song*, and someone else settled it by asking ChatGPT.

**~55s.** Reusable shape: it needs an error, not a villain.

### 🔪 Song Autopsy
Coroner's report on the round's worst-performing song, as a catalogue of wounds. R145's
Bocelli entry: *wound one, familiarity* (Mashew, −1, comment in full: "Played"). *Wound
two, misidentification* — Dave Jensen noticing he submitted the **wrong song**. *Wound
three, class* — Jon: *"Andrea Bocelli is the Appleby's of opera singers."* *Wound four,*
Grant Koziol, −2, comment in full: *"Fuck you."*

**~55s.** Only works on a genuinely dead song.

## Written but not filmed

### 🧾 Receipts
No format at all — four real artefacts in date order, and the joke assembles itself.

> **Aug 2, Second Best.** Mashew downvotes a ska song: *"Ska rule -1, sorry."*
> **Same day, chat.** Sarah Zucker asks what the Ska Rule is, and in the same message names
> the song she almost submitted instead — **No Use For A Name**.
> **Two rounds later.** Mashew submits No Use For A Name. **Wins the round.**
> **Sarah's ballot on it:** 0 points. *"I wish I had a point for this song."*

Nobody in the league noticed this, because noticing it means reading a three-week-old chat
message against a later ballot. **~20s. Possibly the best material in the whole database.**

### 📼 Previously On…
Same juxtaposition, TV-recap framing, hard music cut-out. 100% archive — no current-round
content at all. Good at the *start* of a round to set up a grudge that's still running.

### 🎙️ Dramatic Reading
One real chat message read completely straight by a neutral narrator, then silence. Not an
impression — the comedy is in what the message turns out to be about.

### 🏈 Scouting Report
Sports-desk breakdown of a player's actual tendencies across every round they've played.
Real findings on Mashew: eleven different players downvoted, **never once his wife** (0 for
15), highest rate against Sarah Black, and his stated reasons are almost never musical —
*"no thank you" / "Still don't appreciate" / "Nope" / "Too jokey."* Twice he's admitted on
the ballot that it was random: *"Flipped a tuna can and landed on edge."*

### 📊 The Counterfactual Ballot
Pure arithmetic, no format. *What would the standings be without this one vote?*

> Sarah's ska song finished on 12 — **level with Mashew's own submission**. His was the only
> downvote it got. Remove it and she finishes above him.

Reachable only by doing sums on the vote table. No chat message contains it. **This is the
one thing the bot can do that no human in the league can.**

### 🚫 NO BIT
Not a joke — a decision. The bot looking at a week and saying *there isn't one here.* We
tested it on "the Frank Black Embargo," which sounds like league lore and turns out to be
two chat messages eight minutes apart. **Being willing to say nothing is what stops the rest
from becoming a gimmick.**

## Ideas, not yet built

| | |
|---|---|
| 🚸 **"I Learned It From Watching You"** | The old anti-drug ad. When the bot catches a player calling someone out for a thing they themselves did, it stages the confrontation — *"where did you learn to vote like this?!" / "I learned it from watching you, OK?!"* Needs a real matched pair; we haven't found one yet. |
| 📰 **Breaking News** | Urgent bulletin over a trivial ruling. Works, but needs setup, and it's the most-imitated AI-comedy voice there is. |
| 🎓 **Educational Film** | 16mm classroom short explaining a league behaviour to future generations. Good once, a costume by the third time. |
| 🎧 **Theme Violation Sting** | Five seconds. A game-show buzzer on a dubious theme fit. The whole bit. |
| 🔢 **Single-number bits** | Sometimes the number *is* the joke and needs no format at all. Phil's R137 submission has **23 monthly listeners** on Spotify. The next most obscure song in that round has 484. One card, one number. |
| 💬 **Chat commands** | Reply to any message with `#receipts`, `#court`, `#statement`, `#eulogy`, `#minutes` and get that treatment applied to it on the spot. Also `#canon`, to mark a moment as permanent league lore the bot is allowed to bring back later. |

## The honest part

**What's good:** everything is sourced. No invented quotes, ever — where a bit needs
dialogue (the judge, the counsel) it's labelled parody, and the evidence around it is real.
The formats that work are the ones that add the *least*: the best bit in here is four
screenshots in date order.

**What's unproven:** we have found **two** incidents this good, out of 115 rounds across
six leagues, and we found them by hand. We genuinely don't know yet whether there are five
of these in the whole archive or fifty. That's the difference between *an occasional bit we
make when something great happens* and *a weekly segment* — and until we measure it, the
honest answer is the former.

**What it costs:** the words and the cards are effectively free. Voices are a few cents.
Generated video is ~63¢ per five seconds and **fails maybe half the time** — one of the two
we bought turned the barrister into a completely different person mid-shot. So: sketches
and text, cheap and reliable; moving footage only where a body has to actually do
something.

**The line we're not crossing:** no photorealistic video of real players. The courtroom
sketch is recognisably Jon *and* obviously a drawing, and that's deliberate. Anything that
looks like real footage of someone in this league is a different thing entirely.
